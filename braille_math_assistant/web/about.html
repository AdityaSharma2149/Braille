How This Tool Works

Two solvers, always labeled

Every question is tried first by a solver that runs entirely on this computer: no internet needed, and it cannot make up a wrong answer, because it follows fixed mathematical rules. It handles arithmetic, equations, matrices, and functions like sine, cosine, log, and absolute value — and can explain the answer in English, Hindi, or Tamil on its own.

Only when that solver cannot understand a question (for example, a word problem) does this tool ask an AI model for help. When that happens, the answer is always marked "Unverified — Solved by AI" so you know to double-check it.

What happens to your question

  1. You ask — by typing, speaking, or photographing a question.
  2. Quality check — a photo is checked to make sure the text was read clearly before continuing.
  3. Solving — the local solver tries first; an AI model helps only if needed.
  4. Answer — you get a Braille preview on screen and a slow, spoken answer in your chosen language.

Important limits, stated plainly

  • The Braille shown on screen is a simple preview (English letters and digits only). It is not official Nemeth, UEB, or Bharati Braille, and a screen cannot raise physical dots.
  • Speaking your question works fully offline once the speech model is downloaded the first time.
  • Photographs in poor lighting may not be read correctly — you can always type the question instead.
  • The local solver covers arithmetic, linear and quadratic equations, matrices up to 3 by 3, and functions such as sine, cosine, log, and absolute value. Anything beyond that goes to the AI model and is marked unverified.

Why this tool exists

Commercial Braille displays can cost more than ₹1.5 lakh, putting them out of reach for many students and schools. This tool aims to give blind and low-vision students a free, offline-capable way to work through STEM mathematics — without needing expensive hardware.
